Aurora

Original Hearth artwork, drawn as SVG for this project. No third-party
reference images or artwork were used. design/art/aurora.svg is the original
single-file wallpaper and stays unchanged as the reference; 00-sky.svg and
01-curtain.svg are split from it, and scripts/render-theme.sh compares the
pack's rest state with it.

Weather variants

All variant layers are original work drawn for Hearth from this pack's own
sources, on the same horizon and treeline. Layers that sit on the trees reuse
layer 00's treeline path and displacement filter, so they land exactly on it.
No third-party material. Soft layers render at half resolution; the star, snow
and rime strips are sharp detail and render at full resolution as partial
strips. No base layer changed and nothing is replaced; every variant only adds
layers, so the sky and curtain are shared.

- snow (sky snow): the lit cloud deck from overcast, snow resting on the tree
  tops, a lighter snow-laden forest and a moonlit snowfield in front of it
  that glows blue-lavender, darkening toward the tiles. Sparse soft flakes
  fall slowly (fall 36 dp every 12 s) and read as a still texture with motion
  off.
- snow-coming (clear or cloudy sky, snow forecast): a thin, high pearl veil
  moves in ahead of the snow and the moon wears a soft ring through it. The
  curtain still shows, softened. No flakes.
- fog (sky fog): luminous ground fog pooled along the forest, silvered by the
  moon and faintly green under the curtain. Ground fog forms under a clear
  sky, so the aurora stays.
- freezing-clear: the extra stars of a dry, cold night with a few glinting, a
  clear cool band of light along the treeline, rime on the tree tips and a
  sprinkle of frost glints on the ground.
- cold-clear: the extra stars and the clear treeline band, without frost.
- overcast (sky cloudy, and rain or storm through the sky widening): an
  overcast night hides the aurora, so a broken cloud deck covers it, with the
  cloud undersides catching its teal light and the curtain showing through
  the breaks. Light stays in the sky; the scene is never a flat dark ceiling.

Left out, and why:
- rain: rain needs a thick, low deck that hides the aurora completely, and
  the place it would leave (a black treeline under black cloud) is untrue to
  this scene's light and would be gloomy. Rain and storm widen to cloudy and
  show the overcast variant, which keeps glimpses of the glow.
- hot-clear: an aurora is seen from cold, high-latitude skies, and a hot
  night under one is not true to the place. Hot clear nights show the plain
  scene.

Entries are ordered most specific first: snow, snow-coming (it has a
forecast), fog, freezing-clear, cold-clear, overcast.
