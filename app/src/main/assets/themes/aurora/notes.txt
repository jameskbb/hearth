Aurora

Original Hearth artwork, drawn as SVG for this project. No third-party
reference images or artwork were used. design/art/aurora.svg is the original
single-file wallpaper and stays unchanged as the reference; 00-sky.svg and
01-curtain.svg are split from it, and scripts/render-theme.sh compares the
pack's rest state with it.

Weather variants

All variant layers are original work drawn for Hearth from this pack's own
sources, on the same horizon and treeline. Layers that sit on the trees reuse
layer 00's treeline path and displacement filter, so they land exactly on it.
No third-party material. Soft layers render at half resolution; the star, snow
and rime strips are sharp detail and render at full resolution as partial
strips. No base layer changed and nothing is replaced; every variant only adds
layers, so the sky and curtain are shared.

- snow (sky snow): the lit cloud deck from overcast, broken snow caps of
  varied depth on the upward edges of the crowns (brightest under the
  curtain), a forest band with a soft vertical gradient and a scatter of
  snowy crowns, and a moonlit snowfield in front that glows blue-lavender,
  darkening toward the tiles. Sparse soft flakes of mixed sizes fall slowly
  (fall 36 dp every 12 s) and read as a still texture with motion off.
- snow-coming (clear or cloudy sky, snow forecast): a thin, high pearl veil
  moves in ahead of the snow. The moon shows through it with a small glow
  and a soft 22 degree halo: a faint inner edge with a trace of warmth,
  feathered outward and drawn through the veil's own texture, so it reads
  as part of the cloud. The curtain still shows, softened. No flakes.
- fog (sky fog): luminous ground fog pooled along the forest, silvered by the
  moon and faintly green under the curtain, bleeding past both frame edges.
  Ground fog forms under a clear sky, so the aurora stays.
- freezing-clear: the extra stars of a dry, cold night (dense in the upper
  sky, a spread of sizes, a few with soft cross glints), a deeper and more
  saturated zenith, a clean cyan-teal band of clear air behind the treeline,
  soft broken rime on the crown tips, and a faint frost sheen on the ground
  in front of the trees with sparse sparkles inside it.
- cold-clear: the same stars and clear air at 70% opacity, without frost:
  visibly sharper than the plain scene, gentler than freezing.
- overcast (sky cloudy, and rain or storm through the sky widening): an
  overcast night hides most of the aurora. A broken deck covers it; no cloud
  is darker than the base sky behind it, the undersides catch the curtain's
  teal light, and the deck thins to broken bands across the curtain's
  brightest run so more than half of it shows through. The land takes a
  little of the sky's light, and a faint high haze keeps the gaps near the
  top from reading black.

Left out, and why:
- rain: rain needs a thick, low deck that hides the aurora completely, and
  the place it would leave (a black treeline under black cloud) is untrue to
  this scene's light and would be gloomy. Rain and storm widen to cloudy and
  show the overcast variant, which keeps glimpses of the glow.
- hot-clear: an aurora is seen from cold, high-latitude skies, and a hot
  night under one is not true to the place. Hot clear nights show the plain
  scene.

Entries are ordered most specific first: snow, snow-coming (it has a
forecast), fog, freezing-clear, cold-clear, overcast.
